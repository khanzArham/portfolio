## Brief

A Brick Breaker style arcade game: a paddle, a bouncing ball and a wall of bricks to clear.

I built it to see inheritance, polymorphism and encapsulation working in a program that runs in real time, rather than in small exercises. Writing the game loop, the movement and the paddle and brick collisions myself was the point.

## Method

- Plain C++ with an object-oriented design. I chose C++ for direct control over memory, pointers and how much work each frame does.
- The ball, paddle and bricks are separate classes, so each object owns its own state and drawing and the game loop treats them through a shared interface.
- Each frame, the game moves objects, checks bounding boxes for overlaps, reverses the ball's velocity on impact, and updates the score and game state.

## Failure Log

### The ball passed into the paddle and bricks at high speed

**What happened:** once the ball moved fast enough, it sometimes ended up inside the paddle or a brick and got stuck there instead of bouncing.

**Why:** collisions were checked only at discrete frames, by testing whether two bounding boxes overlapped. At high speed the ball travelled far enough in one frame to land deep inside an object before the check ever saw it.

**What I changed:** I replaced the end-of-frame check with continuous collision detection: the ball's movement for the frame (its velocity scaled by delta time) is tested against the bounding boxes along its whole path, so a hit is found even when one frame would carry the ball through an object. I also reworked how velocity flips on impact, so a fast ball bounces off cleanly instead of sinking in.

## Outcome

A working game, and a practical grounding in:

- designing classes that keep their own state;
- memory allocation with raw and smart pointers;
- structuring a game loop;
- continuous collision detection, and why discrete checks break down at high speed.
